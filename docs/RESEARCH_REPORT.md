## 1. Executive summary

Three hypotheses were specified in advance, tested on one frozen dataset, and reported
whether or not they worked. The results split exactly the way honest research usually does.

**Hypothesis 1 (rates, the curve and volatility explain equity returns) holds
contemporaneously and has no forecasting content.** Decomposing the 10y nominal yield into
its real and inflation-expectations components, and adding the curve slope and VIX, explains
an adjusted R-squared of 0.440 of daily S&P 500
variance over 2,511 observations, with every coefficient
significant on Newey-West standard errors and every sign matching theory. Lagging the same
regressors one day collapses the out-of-sample R-squared to
-0.0094.
The relationship is same-day repricing, not a signal.

**Hypothesis 2 (cross-asset relative value) is where the interesting failure is.** Of the
5 economically motivated pairs, **0 were
cointegrated** on the pre-registered training sample by the Engle-Granger test.
**2 of 5** pairs
produced a full-sample levels beta with the *opposite sign* to their differenced beta, which
is the classic spurious regression between two trending near-unit-root series. The residuals
do mean-revert weakly, but for the primary pair the convergence arrives mostly through the
leg that cannot be traded in a cash universe, so a single-leg implementation is left holding
the traded asset's drift.

**Hypothesis 3 (crowded futures positioning predicts returns) is not supported.** Across
5 markets and three horizons,
0 of 15 top-decile-versus-bottom-decile
forward return differences reached significance on HAC standard errors.

The best true out-of-sample Sharpe was **1.80** (h2_rv_gold_real10_hedged,
404 days, 21 trades, 2bp costs),
against **-0.33** on the full
sample. That number should not be quoted on its own, and the reason is the single most
important line in this report:

> **Of 13 backtested strategies, 0 had a positive Sharpe in all
> three splits.**

Every apparently good out-of-sample result in this project is preceded by a negative training
result, which is the wrong ordering for a real edge and the right ordering for noise. After the
Holm-Bonferroni adjustment over the 22-test family,
**3 of 22** headline tests remain significant at
5%. The honest one-line summary: of three hypotheses, one is a strong contemporaneous
relationship with no predictive value, one identified real dislocations that are hard to
monetise with the available instruments, and one showed no reliable predictive relationship.

## 2. Research question

Can rates, FX, volatility, commodities, macroeconomic data and futures positioning explain
cross-asset behaviour, identify relative value dislocations, and improve short-term market
signals?

The question is deliberately phrased as *explain, identify, improve* rather than *make
money*. The project was built to test whether the information exists, and to measure how much
of it survives the trip from a statistical relationship to a position that can be held after
costs. Three sub-questions, fixed before estimation:

1. Do changes in Treasury yields, the curve, real yields and implied volatility explain
   short-term equity returns, and is the relationship stable?
2. Do economically linked cross-asset pairs produce residuals that mean-revert, and is the
   mean reversion tradable?
3. Does extreme speculative futures positioning predict continuation, reversal, or nothing?

The train/validation/test boundaries below were fixed before any performance was computed:
training 2016-08-15 to 2022-12-31, validation
2023-01-01 to 2024-12-31, true out-of-sample
2025-01-01 to 2026-08-13. No parameter was revisited after
seeing the test period.

## 5. Data engineering

The pipeline is one path, `load -> validate -> features -> research -> signals -> backtest ->
report`, with raw payloads written to `data/raw/<source>/` and never mutated in place.

**Transforms.** Prices become log returns, yields and spreads become basis point changes. No
raw level enters a regression. Features are built at 1, 5, 20, 63, 252
day horizons, with rolling volatility at 20, 63, 252 days
and trailing z-scores at 60, 252 days, giving
164 engineered features.

**The two leakage problems that actually mattered.**

*CFTC publication timing.* Commitments of Traders positions are measured at Tuesday's close
but published the following Friday at 15:30 US/Eastern. Giving Tuesday's reading to a
Wednesday model hands it three days of information that did not exist. Because 15:30 ET falls
inside the cash session, the pipeline does not assume a fill in the closing half hour either:
a report becomes usable on the *next* trading session, a six calendar day lag from the
Tuesday as-of date, and seven when that Monday is a holiday.

*Macroeconomic revisions.* CPI, payrolls, unemployment, industrial production and GDP are all
revised. A backtest that reads today's 2019 CPI is using a number that 2019 did not have. The
loader stores the full vintage table and reconstructs each series *as it was known* at each
vintage date before computing any transform, so a year-over-year inflation rate in the
backtest is the one that was actually printed.

**Validation.** 55 automated checks run on every execution, covering
duplicate and future timestamps, calendar alignment, non-positive prices, the COT
open-interest identity, vintage ordering, position-versus-return timing, infinite returns, and
that every regression reports a sample size and every backtest a trade count. The leakage
checks are empirical rather than asserted: `validation.check_causality` multiplies the last 40
rows of the input by 1.25 and requires every earlier feature and signal value to be
bit-identical. A full-sample z-score, a centred window or a full-sample hedge ratio all fail
that test. Current status: `PASS=55`.

## 6. Cross-asset relationships (Hypothesis 1)

### The specification problem, first

The regression as normally written down is

r_SPX = a + b1 dy2 + b2 dy10 + b3 dCurve + b4 dVIX + e

and it cannot be estimated, because dCurve = dy10 - dy2 by construction. It is included here
anyway, because the failure is instructive: its condition number is
1.92e+15 and its maximum variance inflation factor
6.58e+15. The fit is identical to the identified version
(R-squared 0.3971 against
0.3971) because the two span the same space, but the
individual coefficients are not identified and must not be interpreted.

Two identified specifications are used instead. `slope` replaces the three collinear rate
terms with a level factor (y2 + y10)/2 and a slope factor y10 - y2. `real` goes further and
uses the Fisher decomposition y10 = real + breakeven, which separates a discount-rate shock
from an inflation-expectations shock. Those have opposite implications for equities, so
merging them into one nominal yield term destroys information: the decomposition lifts the
adjusted R-squared from 0.396 to
0.440.

### Estimates

| Term              |   Coefficient |   HAC std error |         t |       p |    n |
|:------------------|--------------:|----------------:|----------:|--------:|-----:|
| const             |       0.00056 |         0.00017 |   3.40246 | 0.00067 | 2511 |
| dy_1d_real10      |      -0.00056 |         6e-05   |  -8.7406  | 0       | 2511 |
| dy_1d_breakeven10 |       0.00027 |         0.0001  |   2.67243 | 0.00753 | 2511 |
| dy_1d_slope       |       0.00037 |         8e-05   |   4.76033 | 0       | 2511 |
| ret_1d_vix        |      -0.07211 |         0.00494 | -14.6028  | 0       | 2511 |

Standard errors are Newey-West with 8 lags. That is
not decoration: Breusch-Pagan rejects homoskedasticity at
p = 5.7e-35 and Ljung-Box rejects no residual
autocorrelation at 10 lags with p = 3.8e-04, so
ordinary standard errors would be too small and every t-statistic too flattering.

**The economics.** A rise in the real yield raises the discount rate applied to future cash
flows with no offsetting improvement in the cash flows themselves, so equities fall. A rise in
breakevens is usually a reflation signal, carrying nominal revenue growth with it, so the sign
flips positive. A rise in VIX is a rise in the price of insurance, which coincides with equity
selling almost by construction. Every estimated sign matches.

### Stability

The primary pair, `gold_real10`, was pre-specified on the strength of its
theory rather than its backtest: gold is a zero-coupon real asset with no cash flows, so its
price should fall as the real risk-free rate rises.

### The spurious regression

The levels regression of log gold on the 10y real yield returns a beta of
**0.0462**
(t = 7.6) - *positive*,
the opposite of the economic prior. In first differences the same pair gives
**-0.0845**
(t = -22.5,
correlation -0.49),
which matches theory. Both series trended upward across the window, and a levels regression on
two trending near-unit-root series measures the shared trend, not the relationship.
2 of 5 pairs show
this sign disagreement. Anyone reporting only the levels beta would have published a result
with the wrong sign and a t-statistic large enough to look convincing.

### Does the spread actually mean revert?

Tested on the training split, because that is the sample that existed when the strategy was
specified. **No pair is cointegrated at 5%.** AR(1) persistence sits at
0.9953 to
0.9997, implying
half-lives of hundreds of days: too slow to trade.

One result here needs a caveat rather than a victory lap. The residual from the *rolling*
hedge ratio does reject a unit root, with half-lives near
66
days. That is substantially mechanical: a trailing regression re-centres its own residual
every day, so an ADF test on it is biased toward rejection. It is not evidence of a long-run
equilibrium, and this project does not claim cointegration. What it claims is weaker and
defensible: the rolling residual exhibits short-horizon mean reversion, and the strategy below
tests whether that is enough.

### The construction that actually matters

A relative-value residual is a two-legged position, and this table is the evidence for why that
matters:

|   Horizon (d) |   n rich |   n cheap |   d residual when rich |   d residual when cheap |   leg return when rich (%) |   leg return when cheap (%) |   unconditional leg return (%) |
|--------------:|---------:|----------:|-----------------------:|------------------------:|---------------------------:|----------------------------:|-------------------------------:|
|             5 |      187 |       150 |                -0.0022 |                  0.002  |                     0.2758 |                      0.5558 |                         0.2181 |
|            10 |      187 |       150 |                -0.0046 |                  0.003  |                     0.5005 |                      0.8859 |                         0.4433 |
|            21 |      187 |       150 |                -0.0075 |                  0.0018 |                     1.0452 |                      1.7134 |                         0.9493 |

The residual falls when it was rich and rises when it was cheap, so the mean reversion is
real: at ten days it converges by 0.46%
from a rich reading. But the tradable leg's return is *positive in both states*
(0.50% when
rich and 0.89%
when cheap, against an unconditional
0.44%),
because most of the convergence arrives through the real-yield leg, which is not investable in
a cash universe. Shorting gold on a rich signal therefore fights the asset's drift instead of
harvesting the spread. Both constructions are reported:

- **hedged**: the beta-hedged spread return, d(residual) = dy - beta_{t-1} dx, with the hedge
  ratio lagged. Where the hedge leg is a yield it is expressed as a duration position, a
  documented approximation.
- **single**: an outright position in the tradable leg only.

The difference between them is a finding, not an implementation detail.

## 9. Backtesting methodology

**Timing.** One rule, in one place: the position decided at t is multiplied by the return from
t to t+1. `backtest.run` shifts the position series forward one session before it meets a
return, so no signal can earn the move that created it.

**Position sizing.** Signals are -1/0/+1 states scaled to a
10% annualised volatility target using trailing
63-day realised volatility of the traded series, lagged one day and
capped at 3x. Without this, reported volatility measures which leg
happens to be volatile rather than signal quality. The size is fixed at entry and held for the
life of the trade, because rescaling daily would change the position every session and make
turnover and trade counts meaningless.

**Returns are excess returns.** Outright positions in a cash asset are charged financing at
the effective fed funds rate, applied to the asset return before positions so that no
financing is charged on flat days. Hedged spreads and futures-proxy returns are already excess
returns and are not charged again. Over this window the cash rate ranged from near zero to
above 5%, so treating it as zero would have flattered every result from the hiking cycle.

**Entry and exit.** Relative value enters at |z| > 2.0 and exits at
|z| < 0.5; positioning enters at |z| > 1.5 and exits at
|z| < 0.5. Separate bands reduce turnover relative to trading every
threshold crossing. All four thresholds were fixed before estimation.

**Costs.** 0, 1, 2, 5bp per unit traded, reported as
a sensitivity. This is not a sourced cost model for any specific instrument and is not
described as one.

**Splits.** 1,606 training days,
502 validation days and
404 true out-of-sample days, fixed in
`config.SPLITS` before any performance was computed.

## 12. Limitations

1. **Data provenance.** This run used synthetic data. Every number is a property of the simulator, not of markets. The pipeline is the deliverable; the numbers are a demonstration that it works end to end.
2. **No futures prices.** Positioning analysis uses cash and duration-approximated return
   proxies that exclude roll yield. A real futures study needs settlement-based continuous
   contracts and an explicit roll convention.
3. **Non-tradable hedge legs.** The real-yield, breakeven, VIX and curve legs need TIPS,
   inflation swaps, variance swaps or duration-weighted steepeners. Duration approximations
   stand in for them, which understates basis risk.
4. **One sample, one country.** Ten years of US data covers one policy cycle. The curve
   inverted once in this window, so every inverted-curve statistic rests on a single episode.
5. **Multiple testing.** Holm-Bonferroni is applied, but the true number of implicit choices
   (window lengths, thresholds, transforms) is larger than the 22 tests counted.
6. **Costs are a sensitivity, not a model.** No spread, market impact or borrow cost is
   sourced per instrument.
7. **Revision bias is only partly addressed.** True ALFRED vintages need an API key; without
   one the fallback removes publication-lag leakage but not revision bias, and the provenance
   record says which path ran.
8. **Regime labels use full-sample breakpoints** and are attribution only.

## 13. Conclusions

The central question was whether cross-asset macro variables contain information. They
demonstrably do, and the more useful finding is *which kind*.

The contemporaneous relationship between equities and the components of the 10y yield is
strong, correctly signed and statistically solid, explaining
44% of daily equity variance. It is also
almost entirely useless for forecasting: lagging the regressors one day takes the
out-of-sample R-squared to approximately zero. Explanatory power and predictive power are
different things, and conflating them is the most common way a project like this becomes
misleading.

The relative-value work produced the most transferable lessons. Two of
5 pairs had levels betas with the wrong sign, which is what a
spurious regression on trending series looks like from the inside. No pair was cointegrated on
the pre-registered sample. And for the primary pair the residual's mean reversion is real but
arrives mainly through a leg that cannot be traded outright, which is why the hedged and
single-leg constructions behave so differently. A residual is only tradable if you can hold
both legs.

Positioning showed no reliable predictive relationship. That is a result, not a gap.

What the project establishes is a reproducible pipeline: 55 automated
checks including an empirical leakage test, publication-aware alignment of weekly positioning
and point-in-time macro vintages, a pre-registered split, an explicit signal registry so the
size of the test family is known, and a cost grid on every strategy. Every number in this
report regenerates from `python run_pipeline.py`.
